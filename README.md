# Motocross RPG: The Road to Legend

A single-player, offline motocross career RPG. Start as an unknown rider with a broken 50cc and work your way up to legend.

## What Alpha v1.1 offers

The game loop: **earn money, keep the bike going, race, level up, repeat.**

1. **Start a career** in Home Town with a broken 50cc. Paul Phoenix, the mechanic at Phoenix MX, gets it running in the tutorial and teaches the basics.
2. **Earn money** on shifts in the Industrial Zone: the fabrication shop (frame brackets) and the engine rebuilder (pistons). Pay is per part made, and is boosted 5× while testing.
3. **Look after the bike.** Every lap wears its parts, and a part at 0% breaks. Buy new or better-grade parts at Moto78 (the bike shop) and the tyre shop in Home Town (fitted free, or kept as spares in your bag), and pay the local garage to repair or fit them. On a shop's Sell tab, tick spares and use **Sell selected**, or **Sell all** (Boss, 31 Oct 2026).
4. **Get a van** and load your bike to reach the tracks on the venue road. On the Old Rusty Truck you can see it: your bike rides in the open bed in its brand colours, strapped down to the front of the bed (Boss, 30 Oct 2026).
5. **Race.** Three venues are open: **Farmland MX**, **Riverside Arena** and **Longmoor Enduro**, with Joseph Halden at the race office of each: his family runs every track in the land, and he introduces himself the first time you talk to him (Boss, 30 Oct 2026). A race day is two motos against 15 riders (5 laps a moto, or 8 at Longmoor Enduro, and 8 in a motocross or endurance championship round), including the Local Champion: £20 entry (your first race day is free), gate picks, a start official and a pit board, with prize money to 10th in each moto. Free practice is 25% of the entry, and the backyard loop at home is free.
6. **Win the badge.** After your first race day, enter the **Local Champion Championship** at any race office: three rounds, one at each track, against the same 16 riders, with points for every moto. Win it for the Area 1 badge. The six-round **Area 1 Championship** is listed but locked until Area 2 is ready (Boss, 29 Oct 2026); its prize money stays as it is until the game is further along.
7. **Level up.** Racing and practice train your Rider Ability skills, and better skills and better parts make you faster.
8. **Race your own tracks** with **Mod Tracks** on the venue road (see *Making and installing mod tracks* below).
9. **Bring your own 3D models** from Blender: swap the scenery, the race bike and the rider, or add new scenery (see *Your own 3D models* below).

It runs in a browser on desktop or on a phone held sideways, and saves automatically.

## Play it

- **In a browser:** once GitHub Pages is switched on (Settings > Pages > Source: GitHub Actions), every push to `main` publishes the game to this repo's Pages site. The v1.5 test build is published beside it at `/test-v1.5/` from the `test/v1.5` branch, with its own saves.
- **On your own computer:** install [Node.js](https://nodejs.org) (LTS), then:

  ```
  npm install
  npm run dev
  ```

  and open the address it prints (usually http://localhost:5173).

### Playing on a phone

Hold the phone sideways: the game is laid out for landscape, and asks you to turn the phone if it is upright. For the most room, use the browser's Add to Home Screen: the game then opens full screen with no browser bars. The game draws at the phone's full screen resolution, and panels shrink to fit a short screen.

### Backing up and moving your career

Your career is saved in the browser you play in, so another browser or device starts empty. **Load → Export save** gives you your career as a file (`mxrpg-<rider>-<date>.save.json`): a download on a computer, the share sheet on a phone or tablet (Save to Files on iPhone and iPad, Drive or Downloads on Android). **Load → Import save** on any browser or device picks that file and carries on the career there; Load is open even with no career saved, for this. Importing over a career keeps the one it replaces as Backup 1 under Load. A save from an older build is upgraded on import like any other; one from a newer build, or a damaged one, is refused with the reason. Export now and then as a backup: Safari can clear a website's storage after about a week without a visit (a Home Screen app is not affected). The code is in `src/core/saves.ts` (the file format) and `src/ui/files.ts`.

**Old saves and v1.5** (Boss, 30 Oct 2026). Careers saved before v1.5 don't carry over: the venues are now one map per track type (`venue-motocross`, `venue-supercross`, `venue-enduro`) and the old venue maps are gone, so a save from Alpha v1.1 or an earlier v1.5 test build may load in the wrong place or not at all. Start a new career for v1.5. This is an Alpha, and old saves aren't kept working between test versions.

## Making and installing mod tracks

Mod tracks are race tracks made with the game's track editor and raced in the game from **Mod Tracks** on the venue road. A mod race day plays exactly like Farmland MX: the same entry fee, field, AI riders, prize money, XP and parts wear. Picking a mod track goes straight to the gate pick, without a venue area to walk round, and race day is the only mode (no practice): that is how mod tracks stay (Boss, 29 Oct 2026), with no modders' town area planned.

**1. Build the track** on a computer: the track editor needs a mouse and keyboard and isn't usable on a phone (racing mod tracks on a phone works, through Import track file). Open the track editor: `tools/track-editor.html` on the published site, or http://localhost:5173/tools/track-editor.html when running `npm run dev`. Choose **New**, draw the route, then add:

- a **start straight**, which needs its own gate and must join the lap;
- a **finish line**;
- a **start board official**, with a walk that ends clear of the track.

Then use **Ready to race**, which runs the game's checks.

**2. Save it.** The editor opens on the mod tracks: the example in `public/mods/tracks/` and any saved there; the game's own four tracks are not listed (the Development edition has a **Show the game's own tracks** tick under the list, remembered in the browser, for working on the game itself). Tracks made with **New**, **Duplicate** or **Import** are mod tracks. The first **Save** asks for the track's name, and the track is saved as its own file, `public/mods/tracks/<name>.track.json`, never into the game's `tracks.json`. It can't overwrite another track: a name already in use is refused.

- With `npm run dev`, use the track editor at http://localhost:5173/tools/track-editor.html and **Load the tracks from this server**. Save then writes straight into `public/mods/tracks/`, and the game picks the track up on its next page load. Saved mod tracks are listed in the editor too, to carry on editing.
- If you **Open folder…** on the project in Chrome or Edge, Save writes into the `public/mods/tracks/` folder there.
- Anywhere else, Save downloads the file for you to put in `public/mods/tracks/`.
- **Autosave** (the tick by Save) saves your changes every two minutes without asking, wherever Save could write quietly: mod tracks through the dev server or into the opened folder. A new track needs its name from Save first, and a download-only setup still needs Save.

**Export** still saves any track as a `.track.json` to share.

**3. Race it.** On your own copy, a track in `public/mods/tracks/` shows under **Mod Tracks** in the game. For a built copy, rebuild with `npm run build`, because the build writes the folder's `index.json` (a website can't list a folder by itself). With `npm run dev`, **Race it in the game** in the editor opens a race day on a saved mod track straight away. On any copy, including the published site, you can also use **Mod Track → Import track file** in the game to pick a `.track.json` from your device. It is kept in that browser only, and can be removed from its race office. `farmland-example.track.json` is in the folder as an example.

**What a mod track must meet.** The game runs the same checks as on its own tracks:

- it is a track file from the editor (or a bare track), and is not marked as an unfinished draft;
- it is not practice-only;
- it has a start straight with its gate, long enough and joined onto the lap;
- every section type is one the game knows;
- it uses only the game's own surfaces, ground and scenery (custom art isn't supported yet);
- the start official stands and walks clear of the track, the line-up and solid scenery.

**Enduro Sprint tracks (Boss, 29 Oct 2026).** Set the track's Kind to **Enduro Sprint** for a point-to-point timed test instead of a loop. There is no start gate or start straight: riders leave the start line one at a time, 10 seconds apart, and can catch and pass the rider in front. After the finish line they roll down a single-file **return lane** (a quarter of the track's width, at half speed, no passing) and queue at the start for the next lap. The return lane's time is left out, and the lowest total time over the timed laps wins. A one-lap timed qualifying sprint sets moto 1's start order (fastest first), and moto 2 goes in moto 1's order. Sprint motos are 8 laps (`kindLaps` in `racing.json`). In the editor, place the finish line, then use **Start line** to lay the return lane from the finish to the start; the track's checks then ask for:

- the return lane as the first section, at least 20 grid units long, kept clear of the test except at its ends;
- no start straight and no start board official;
- the sprint start (an arch with a timing beam, a clock, a timekeeper with a stopwatch and a scorer with a clipboard) on the start line, which is added for you if it's left out.

Enduro sections that any mod track can use: **rocks**, **logs**, **log step**, **water crossing**, **roots**, **off-camber**, **hill climb** and **woods**, with **boulder** and **log pile** scenery. Endurocross obstacles (Boss, 1 Nov 2026), for sprints, cross and endurance races alike: **concrete pipe** (one big pipe across the track, mud packed up its sides), **tyre bed** (tractor tyres on their sides over the whole section), **tyre wall** (two rows of upright machinery tyres packed with mud) and **log stack** (two 3-2-1 log triangles, up and over). The rock garden is rock edge to edge, and cut rounds fill the gaps between the logs of a log crossing. Through rough going riders wander: in on one line, out on another, or across and back. The example sprint is **Quarry Sprint** (`public/mods/tracks/quarry-sprint.track.json`). Mod tracks could use it first (Boss, 29 Oct 2026); the game's own sprints are in Areas 5 to 8 (Boss, 4 Nov 2026).

**No-Turn Takedown tracks (Boss, 30 Oct 2026).** Set the track's Kind to **No-Turn Takedown** for a straight drag strip on a long, slim **200 × 30** grid (half the motocross grid's short side by twice its long side). There are no bends or corners anywhere: the editor never lets you draw the route by hand. Pick **Start platform** under Start and finish and click where the start line goes; the run goes straight on from there to the finish line, and **+ Straight** / **− Straight** make it longer or shorter, 10 grid units at a time. Jumps, whoops, rhythm and the rest go on the run as features (no corner features), and a tabletop or double at the end of the run can be the finish line, with the finish arch over it, as on a supercross track. Past the finish line is a short **run-off** where riders pull up (the first section); the way back from it to the start is never drawn or ridden. The start is a **raised platform** with four gates, two each side of the **starter's box**, and riders drop down a short hill onto the run, with no first corner.

A No-Turn Takedown race day is a **knockout of 32 riders**: everyone rides a timed **seeding run** on their own, 8 seconds apart. You go second, so only one rider is ahead of you, and once you have your time the rest of the field's runs are done off screen and the bracket comes up with your time and seed (Boss, 31 Oct 2026). There is no Auto Race on this track type, as every run is a single short one; the seeding times split the field into **heats of four**, the fastest kept apart, and the **first two** in each heat go through (32 → 16 → 8 → 4). The heats are drawn up as a fixed **bracket**, two-sided like a printed tournament bracket with the final in the middle (Boss, 30 Oct 2026): each heat's first two go on to the box beside it in the next round, and seeds 1 and 2 start on opposite sides, so they can only meet in the final. The starter's box between the gates on the heat screen is a plain black box. The **final** is the best of three runs: each run scores its place (1 for a win, 4 for last) and the lowest total wins, the last run breaking a tie. Riders out earlier are placed by the round they went out in, then their place in their heat, then their seeding time. Prize money is two motos' worth at your overall place plus the podium bonus. The rules are `sim.knockout` in `racing.json`. The CPU riders' own heats are run for you between yours. It is **late-game content**, balanced to be as hard to win as a race day at the same level (Boss, 30 Oct 2026): the field is the **31 best riders** in the pool, raced **50% better** (skills and bike condition, `buff`), and a run rides with a fifth of the usual luck (`noise` 0.2) because it is only a few sections long, so skill counts as much as over a moto's laps. Measured over 300 days on a bike at 90% (`npx vite-node tools/sim/knockout.ts`), a rider wins it about as often as a Farmland race day: knockout 0% / race day 0% at rider level 1, 1–3% / 1% at level 10, 12% / 9–11% at 15 and 37–40% / 25–35% at 20. Getting on the podium is a little harder than at a race day, as you have to reach the final. The pool is the Area 1 rider pool, now 50 riders: 21 of them (`cpu.r30` to `cpu.r50` in `riders.json`) are placeholders added for the knockout, to be replaced with real riders later. The example is **Takedown Strip** (`public/mods/tracks/takedown-strip.track.json`). It was for mod tracks only at first (Boss, 30 Oct 2026); the game's own strips are in Areas 6 to 8 (Boss, 4 Nov 2026).

**Supercross nights (Boss, 1 Nov 2026).** Every Supercross race day and championship round, at every stadium track in every area (Riverside Arena too), runs the **AMA night programme** with no qualifying. **32 riders** (31 CPU riders and you) are drawn at random into **two heats of 16**: the **first 6** in each heat go through to the **Main Event**, the **last 2 go home**, and the rest (8 from each heat) ride the **LCQ** (Last Chance Qualifier), whose **first 4** make the Main. You are drawn into Heat 1 or Heat 2, and the other heat (and the LCQ, if you went straight through) is run off screen and its results shown. Heats pick gates in a random draw; the LCQ in heat finishing order; the Main gives the heat winners first pick, then the heats' 2nds and so on, with the LCQ's four last (a tie goes to the faster race time). Heats are 4 laps, the LCQ 3 and the Main 8 (`sim.supercross` in `racing.json`). **Only the Main pays and scores**, and it is worth a whole race day: two motos' prize money at your Main finish plus the podium bonus, and in a championship two motos' points (Boss, 1 Nov 2026). Knocked out in a heat or the LCQ, your night ends with no prize or points and the entry fee gone, but you keep the XP and the wear from what you rode. **Leave early** is there between races (it counts as not making the Main). **Between races you can repair at the track** with your own tools: Bike check has a Repair button on each part your tools cover, free, back to 100% and one grade down as at home. A championship's Supercross round races its own riders plus enough of the area's other riders (buffed like the rest) to make 32. The rules are `src/core/supercross.ts`.

**Move whole track** (any Kind) picks the whole track up as one piece in the editor and drops it somewhere else on the grid, with its start, features, scenery, ground paint and heights.

A track that fails stays in the list, and picking it shows the reasons. An import that fails isn't added. Each track is listed by its name, with its file name under it. Mod tracks get their own ids, so they never clash with the game's tracks. The code is in `src/core/modTracks.ts`.

## Your own 3D models (Blender)

Every object in the race view can be swapped for your own model, and new scenery added (Boss, 31 Oct 2026). From v2.0 Boss's models are the game's own, built-in models for nearly every object (93 `.glb` files in `public/assets/models/`: the race bikes and rider, the 50cc mini, pit bike, enduro and vintage twin-shock bikes on stands (Boss, 3 Nov 2026), marshals, flag men and pit crews, the paddock vehicles, the finish arches, the TV crews, stands and crowds, and the trackside and pit scenery; Boss, 2 Nov 2026), and the old objects built from boxes in code that they replaced are gone. Still built in code: the start gate and sprint start (they stretch to the track), the skip (Boss kept the game's own),  the start official's box, the stands' and moving figures' frames the models' parts are fitted to, and the race bike the game builds if its model is missing. An item whose model is missing shows as a plain box. `public/mods/models/` is now empty, for your own models, which replace the game's own of the same name. Open **Models** from the toolkit (or the track editor's **Models…** button, or the game with `?models`), pick an object and **Export** it as a `.glb`. Open it in Blender, change it, export it as glTF Binary into `public/mods/models/`, and reload. A file named after a scenery item (`hay_bale.glb`) replaces it on every track; `race_bike.glb`, `race_bike_youth.glb` and `race_rider.glb` replace the race bike and rider (and `sidecar.glb` is every outfit in a sidecar race; `stand_pallet.glb`, `stand_tower.glb`, `stand_bales.glb` and `pit_wall.glb` are the marshals', flag men's and pit crews' stands for each design, in the item's colour through materials named Team and TeamDark, Boss, 1 Nov 2026; and `paddock_stand.glb` is the stand under every bike on a stand (the item, and those under awnings and in pit tents), which then carries your race bike in the item's design and colour), painted in each rider's colours through materials named Plastics, Frame, Plate, Helmet, Jersey and so on; any other name becomes a new item in the track editor's Scenery palette. The solo rider moves on the bike (Boss, 3 Nov 2026): Boss's rider model carries poses built from real riding references, which the race blends as riders go round, stood up in the attack position over bumps and whoops, sat with the inside leg out through slow corners, leaning in stood up on berms and fast corners, back on the brakes, forward on the gas, compressed into a jump, neutral in the air, soaking up the landing, feet down at the gate and sat back in a wheelie (`src/core/riderPose.ts`). The marshals, flag man, pit crews, fans and start official take a model part by part: name the parts in Blender (Body, Arm, LegL and so on, origins on their joints) and the game moves them as it moves its own (Boss, 31 Oct 2026). A scenery model can also carry its own animation, which plays on a loop. Parts named **Track** follow one rider a lap, picked at random, as they go by (TV cameras, photographers; Boss, 3 Nov 2026, so a camera no longer swings between riders in a group), **Flash** blinks as a rider passes (camera flashes), and **Drone** flies off after a random rider as the field comes by and returns to its pilot for the next pass. In a stand or crowd, each **Cheer** section is given a rider at random each lap and stands up only as that rider goes by, and **Supporter** fans dotted through different sections jump up for the same few favourite riders, in place of the Mexican wave (Boss, 1 and 2 Nov 2026). The start gate and sprint start stretch to fit the track, so they stay as they are; the finish arch takes `finish_arch_mx.glb` and `finish_arch_sx.glb`, built in parts the game moves out and stretches to the track's width (Boss, 1 Nov 2026). A model that breaks a rule is skipped (the game's own model shows) and the Models page says why. The rules for each type (size, triangles, ground, facing, colour slots) are in [docs/MODELS.md](docs/MODELS.md); the code is `src/core/modModels.ts`, `src/world/modModels.ts` and `src/scenes/ModelsScene.ts`. Banners and sponsor boards show the words typed for them, or a PNG of your own picked in the track editor under **Picture** from `public/mods/banners/` (best 2000 × 200 pixels for a banner, 1200 × 260 for a sponsor board; an example of each is there; Boss, 1 Nov 2026); a banner or board model shows them on its material named Text. Every other item with its own words takes a picture the same way, on the game's own item and on a model's Text material: feather flags (tall, 200 × 1000; a wide picture is turned to read upwards there, as the words are), the sponsor inflatable, the finish arch, the sprint start, the podium, holeshot board and stadium tunnel, and the tents', vans' and stands' name boards (Boss, 2 Nov 2026; the sizes are in that folder's README.txt and the Picture box).

## Sidecar racing (test v1.5, Boss, 1 Nov 2026)

Sidecars are coming as a class of their own, brought in late in the game: no new venues, they race only on Motocross tracks once unlocked, and they will open championships across the land for their class. **Players can't race them yet** (Boss, 1 Nov 2026: "Must remove the ability to test race the Sidecars, they will be enabled later"): the Farmland race office's test tick box is gone. They can ride in one, though: as **Ray Crispy's passenger** (below, Boss 4 Nov 2026). The racing itself is built and tested:

- Under `npm run dev` only, `?testtrack=farmland&sidecar` jumps straight into a TEST sidecar race: you on a sidecar against 7 others, two motos of the laps a 50cc races there. It is free and counts for nothing: no prize money or XP, no wear, no records (you borrow a stock outfit for the day).
- The start: 8 outfits, 4 on the front row and 4 on the back, each taking two gates. An outfit sits on an even gate (2, 4, 6, 8 in front, 10 to 16 behind) with its chair over the odd gate beside it, so only the even gates can be picked; the odd ones show "chair".
- An outfit never whips or scrubs, whatever its rider's skill, and never loops out at the gate. It stays flat through corners, and once clear of the gate it keeps its bike far enough from the left edge for the chair to stay on the track, with more room between outfits than between solo bikes.
- Boss's `sidecar.glb` in `public/assets/models/` is every outfit, painted in each team's colours (Plastics, Frame, Plate); without it the game draws its own bike with a chair.
- The crew: `sidecar_crew.glb` holds the rider and passenger together, two objects named **Rider** and **Passenger**, each with its origin at its hips, at most 8,000 triangles, in the team's gear (Helmet, Visor, Jersey, Pants, Boots, Gloves). In a left-hander the passenger hangs out over the chair, low and leaning out; in a right-hander they climb across over the back of the bike; the rider leans a little into each turn; and both tuck down and forward over the jumps (`crewPose` in `RaceView.ts`). A figure can carry its own poses as shape keys named **Left**, **Right** and **Jump** (the passenger hanging out over the chair, across on the back of the seat, crouched holding the grab rail; Boss's video and photos, 1 Nov 2026): the game blends them in instead of moving the whole figure. Without the file, sidecars carry the race rider and no passenger.

The rules are in `src/core/sidecar.ts` (gates, MX-only, the borrowed outfit), the `sidecar` class in `bikes.json` (`"sidecar": true`, after the solo ladder; no solo class moves up to it, and its unlock of 99 holds its place until Boss sets one) and `racing.json`, the `sidecar` option on `RaceDay` and `MotoSim`, and the director's sidecar mode in `riderAI.ts`. - **Track raised and lowered along its length (elevation tool overhaul):** the Height pane's main tools now raise and lower a stretch of the track rather than painting the ground under it. The old brush painted a ground lattice two grid units apart and the track took its level from the ground under its centre, so uneven dabs beside the centre line pushed the edge vertices up cell by cell (the torn, stepped edge), the ground eased out to a raised track over a fixed 3 units however high it was (a near-vertical mud wall) and every dab piled onto the last. Now a raise or lower is stored on the track as a hill (where along the route, how long, how high in metres), the track's level is the ground under its middle plus its hills, the hill's shape is a raised cosine that is flat at its peak and at both ends, and the grass ground either side is graded to the track's level at the foot of its bank and runs down to the land at no more than 1 in 1.5. The surface model, the race view's mesh and terrain, collision, the lap profile and grades, the steep-track warning, the height limit check, the rider AI, saving and reloading, route edits and the start straight's join all follow the hills. Tracks without hills are unchanged and the shipped tracks export byte-identical.

- **Fixed sections, five moulding points, the build boundary, cheering fans and race incidents (refinement 4):** once placed, a section never changes: a new piece joins an open end and leaves every earlier section's points, metres and type exactly as they were, and on a piece-built lap dragging a point re-measures only the sections that point belongs to (a shared join lengthens one and shortens the other, and nothing beyond either moves). The rider who crashed is shaken for the rest of that lap, 8% slower in the next section and fading to nothing over four (`sim.afterCrash` in `racing.json`); the player's crashes work the same way. A rider held up behind one they couldn't pass is still held (the simulation says how long, `held` on the failed pass), and the feed still only mentions a failed pass when the rider was on the wheel. **Fluid riding (Boss, 4 Nov 2026: "I want fluid racing, no stutters, no instant slowdowns or stops unless that rider has crashed"; 3 Nov: "flow smoothly round the track gaining and losing speed realistically"):** the simulation still decides every section time, but the rider AI no longer rides each section at that section's own speed (which had bikes bursting through a short straight, slowing in the air and all but freezing behind a rider they couldn't pass). Each lap is now planned as one **speed profile per rider** (`src/core/ridePlan.ts`): a speed at every few metres of the lap, going faster on the straights and slower in the corners by the section's own shape, never accelerating harder than 5 m/s² or braking harder than 8, holding a constant speed through the air, never below walking pace unless the rider is down, and the only stop is a crash. The profile is fitted so the rider still covers each section in the simulation's time, and the lap's start and finish stay exactly on the simulation's marks; a section's own mark may be crossed a few tenths of a second early or late, and a held-up rider may be drawn alongside the rider who held them for a moment. `npx vite-node tools/sim/fluidity.ts [seeds] [tracks…]` races every track with every rider and faults any backwards step, stop, air-speed change or burst harder than those limits; the day's build ran 1,000 races clean. ### Technology

TypeScript, [Phaser 3](https://phaser.io) and [Vite](https://vitejs.dev), with [Vitest](https://vitest.dev) for tests. Saves are JSON in the browser's storage; a desktop app wrapper (Tauri) with saves as files comes in a later build. None of the Adventure Land code is used.

### Updating the art

When the art threads change a map or sprite, run the import tool against the shared art folder and commit the result:

```
npm run import-art -- /path/to/art
npm test
```

To update only the van sheets (from `art/vehicles/sheets/map/`) without re-baking the maps: `npm run import-art -- --vehicles /path/to/art`.

### The toolkit

The editors are kept apart from the game as the **Motocross RPG Toolkit**: its front page is `tools/index.html`, published at `…/tools/` beside the game (http://localhost:5173/tools/ with `npm run dev`), listing the **Track Editor** and the **Map Editor**, with the **Outfit Designer** and **Character Designer** to come. How to use each editor, mode by mode, is in [docs/TOOLKIT_INSTRUCTIONS.md](docs/TOOLKIT_INSTRUCTIONS.md) (the toolkit's own instructions, linked from its front page). The track editor builds everything from motocross tracks to supercross (triples, step-downs, dragon's backs, tunnels through a tabletop with the lap riding under it, podiums, pyro and a stadium tunnel). Its panels dock, resize and pop out, with a Scenery shelf of the items as the race view draws them and Ready to race in the status bar (Boss, 2 Nov 2026). The map editor's Map tab is laid out the same way, its checks in the status bar and the Palette along the bottom, and both editors autosave every two minutes when they can write into the game (Boss, 2 Nov 2026). To add a tool, put its page in `tools/`, add a card to `tools/index.html`, and add its file to `TOOLKIT_PAGES` in `vite.config.ts` so the build publishes it. On Windows, double-click `Toolkit.cmd` to open it as its own window: it checks for Node.js, installs the packages the first time, starts the dev server silently and opens the toolkit in Edge or Chrome's app mode (`tools/toolkit.ps1`).

### Races on screen

The race simulation (`src/core/race.ts`) decides every result. The rider AI (`src/core/riderAI.ts`) then acts the race out: each rider picks a line, brakes into corners and drives out, swings out to pass or tucks in behind, jumps, skims whoops, crashes and remounts, always reaching the end of each section when the simulation says. Riders carry their speed from one section into the next, braking into the slow ones and driving out onto the straights, so speed never jumps at a section's edge (Boss, 2 Nov 2026: no slowing then a burst of speed). Out of corners and down long straights riders sometimes lift the front in a small power wheelie, never a 12 o'clock, its height different each time and harder riders doing it more often (`src/core/wheelie.ts`; Boss, 2 Nov 2026); sidecar outfits keep all three wheels down. A jump face or hill raised above its type's usual height slows riders up it and adds to the section's time, as if the section were longer (`src/core/climb.ts`; Boss, 2 Nov 2026), so the supercross tracks' taller jumps cost a couple of percent on the lap. Good jumpers tyre tap through doubles, triples, quads, rhythms and dragon's backs: they land only the back wheel on a hump's top, front wheel still up, and pop straight off it into the next hop, with no trick thrown into the tap (`tap` ways in `src/core/jumps.ts`; Boss, 2 Nov 2026). Through whoops riders skim the tops, hold a proper wheelie right across (front up about 24° to 34°, the back wheel skimming the tops and the nose bobbing on each, as in Boss's Daytona clip), or, if they jump well, jump them in rhythm two or three whoops a hop, tapping the back wheel on a top between hops; rollers are skimmed or wheelied (`WHOOP_WAYS`, `ROLLER_WAYS` in `src/core/jumps.ts`; Boss, 2 Nov 2026). The track editor has a **Bomb hole** section, a hole dug into the ground with a jump lip on the way in: skilled riders clear it off the lip, others drop in and jump up and out the far side, and a few roll through; its Height is how deep it is (Boss, 2 Nov 2026). On the big jumps the field's best jumper (the showman, in every race) and other skilled jumpers throw whips and scrubs, and one thrown on a hop where the simulation has them make a mistake ends in a crash or a sketchy landing. You get them with your Rider Ability level (Boss, 30 Oct 2026): whips from level 30, rarely at first and up to 70% of big hops at level 70, and scrubs from level 60, up to 70% at level 99. The level builds in the background, but you can't throw either until the Rider Trainer (an NPC planned for Area 3) teaches them (save flag `rider_trainer_tricks`, `TRICKS_FLAG` in `tricks.ts`), so for now only the CPU riders do. How each rider rides comes from their skills, their bike and their race message (`src/core/riderProfile.ts`), plus an optional `style` for CPU riders in `riders.json` (`line`, `aggression`, `jumps`). The race view (`src/world/RaceView.ts`) draws it all as an isometric 3D scene with [three.js](https://threejs.org), in the spirit of Super Off Road. The track is a solid surface (`src/core/trackSurface.ts`): jumps have take-off faces, tables and landings, whoops and braking bumps ripple, berms bank up, hills rise out of the ground, and a raised lip and earth bank mark both edges. Each bike's wheels are put on that same surface, so bikes pitch over jumps, lean into corners and land on the track. The camera follows your rider (the orange one with the arrow), or the leader once you are out, like a TV camera: it pans after them smoothly and only slowly follows their height, so jumps and bumps don't bounce the picture (Boss, 1 Nov 2026), and a map in the corner shows the whole lap, the start straight and every rider. Tall scenery such as stands and trees fades when a rider is behind it.

The race screen is the track view plus a side column (Boss, 31 Oct and 1 Nov 2026). The view runs from the top of the window down to the bottom of the Retire and Details buttons. Over its top-left corner, in outlined text, are the race line (track, moto or session, and Auto Race when it is on; the button was called Auto-complete until Boss renamed it on 2 Nov 2026), then your position, lap and bike (the Ahead and Behind gap lines went on 3 Nov 2026, Boss: the standings show the gaps). A small binoculars button in the view's bottom-right corner cycles the camera through ×2 and ×3 and back to the usual view, still following you (Boss, 3 Nov 2026; ×4 and ×5 were tried and dropped the same day). Under the lap map are your pit board message and the pen, both in the orange of the picked speed button (Boss, 2 Nov 2026; blue before). The race log sits over the bottom-left corner, one size smaller, five lines at most, newest at the top: each new line comes in at the top and pushes the older ones down (Boss, 2 Nov 2026). The longest line it can write splits over two lines. The side column is only as wide as the standings need. Your row is highlighted in place of an arrow, and the times sit just after the field's longest name. Under it are the speed buttons (Pause is the two-bar sign) and Retire and Details, edge to edge. Skip to results shows at the view's bottom-right once you finish or are out.

The race opens on the start straight: the field lines up behind the gate on a concrete pad, wider than the track so a row of 8 fits, in two rows of 8 for a full field, and the straight narrows as it runs onto the lap, where the lap's lip and bank open up so the two surfaces merge.

The gate you pick is your slot: gates 1 to 8 are the front row from one end to the other, 9 to 16 the second row behind them, and riders arrive at the line-up in gate order. A gate past the field's size, or one already taken, is refused rather than swapped.

The start procedure is the same for a Race and for Auto Race. When the race opens, 4 front-row riders are already in their slots (more for a bigger field) and the rest wait scattered just behind the pad. Over about 6 seconds they ride up to their own slots at different moments and speeds, several at once, easing onto their lane line, without touching. A second-row rider only sets off once the front-row rider in their own lane has stopped in their slot, so second-row riders in lanes that are already filled go early while other lanes are still filling. The line-up is drawn from a seed made of the track and the gate order, so each moto lines up differently but the same race always looks the same. An official (drawn at 90% of their first size, board included) waits beside the straight near the gate (A) from the moment the race screen opens, with the START 30 board low and its plain white back out. When everyone is still and the 5-second hold starts they raise it face out and walk up the straight to B, upright at 5 and 4, turned sideways at 3 and 2, and lower it white side out at 1, just before the gate drops; they stay at B with it lowered for the rest of the race. A, B and the whole walk are planned per track (`src/core/startOfficial.ts`) clear of the track, its lip and slope, and the scenery, with room for every turn of the board, and a track with nowhere clear fails the checks. There is no countdown on screen; the board and the riders show it. The procedure runs in real time at any playback speed, and Pause stops it. Practice has no line-up or board.

**The supercross finish (Boss, 1 Nov 2026).** At Supercross, EnduroCross and No-Turn Takedown tracks, a finish-line jump's arch is the stadium gantry, with the flag man up in a scaffold tower beside it, flagging the white and chequered flags as a placed flag man does; he is the track's flag man, so the track editor won't place another there (ticking Finish line on a jump takes a placed one away, and Checks marks one as an error). Elsewhere the arch is the motocross truss arch. Both use Boss's models when they're in the models folder (`finish_arch_sx.glb`, `finish_arch_mx.glb`); without them the game builds its own arch and a roofed tower.

At Supercross, EnduroCross and No-Turn Takedown tracks, fireworks go off over the finish arch as the winner finishes (Boss, 30 Oct 2026): rockets go up from the tops of the arch's posts a little before the winner crosses the line and burst over the track for about six seconds (`src/core/fireworks.ts`). They run on the race clock, so Pause holds them, and a track with no arch sends them up from either side of the finish line.

After the flag each finisher rides a slow cool-down lap down the nearer edge of the track, in single file behind the finisher ahead on that edge, giving way to riders still racing (before, every finisher stopped in one heap on the track about 40 m past the line at Farmland, where riders still racing rode through them).

Riders sit on the race-day bike model from the vehicles thread (Boss, 25 Oct 2026: "use the model from the coloured race day rides"): a youth bike for the 50 to 85 classes and an adult bike above, in the bike's brand colours, with the rider in their gear colours (`src/world/raceBike.ts`). It only changes how a rider looks. The tyres touch the ground exactly where the old bike's wheels did, so every rider takes the same line round every track as before.

Scenery may not stand on the racing surface, the start straight included; the game's checks and the track editor's **Checks** refuse it (see the toolkit instructions).
